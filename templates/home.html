04 / Safety toolkit

Don't memorise. Remember these.

Cyber safety isn't “never go online”. It's knowing which moment deserves a three-second pause. Open a card for the how.

Password managerOne vault. A different password everywhere.

Hand your passwords to a dedicated vault and remember one strong master phrase. Your browser's built-in manager is enough to start; 1Password or Bitwarden are solid next steps.

Tonight: pick your most important account and replace the reused password with a long, generated one.

Two-step verification2FA · one more lock if your password leaks

After your password, you need a second key. Authenticator apps or passkeys (fingerprint, face) are safer than SMS.

Order: email → banking → social. Your email can reset almost everything else, so lock it first.

Updates patch holesNot nagging. Patching.

System, browser and app updates fix known flaws. Scammers love a device that hasn't been updated.

Habit: turn on automatic updates and install them while charging. Restart once so they finish.

The 3-2-1 backupDon't keep all your eggs in one basket.

3 copies, on 2 different kinds of media, with 1 off-site. Ransomware fears a good backup.

Careful: syncing isn't backing up. Delete a file and the cloud deletes it too. Use a service with version history.

Verify before you actUrgency, fear and greed are the accelerators.

Hang up. Close the message. Call back on the official number you find, or open the official app. Never use the link or number they gave you.

Remember: there is no “safe account”, no regulator asks you to transfer money, and no support agent needs your SMS code.

AI deepfakesFaces and voices can be faked.

The person on the video may not be who they appear to be. Ask them to turn their head, make a gesture, or say something only you two know. Or hang up and call back on another channel.

Family code: agree on a passphrase. Any request involving money, investments or urgent transfers gets the code first.

05 / For parents & teachers

Questions aren't the problem. Going it alone is.

This island doesn't test memory — it trains judgement. Adults don't need to be technical. They just need to play one round.

10 minute family challenge

  1. Let them beat you first. Play Phishing Detective together. Don't correct immediately — let them point out the tells.
  2. Ask a perspective flip. “If you were the scammer, how would you do it? Who's the easiest target?”
  3. Turn on 2FA together. Pick email or social, scan the QR code, save the backup codes.
  4. Agree on a family code. For example, “I feel like mango shaved ice” means “I'm in trouble, help me” — and only family knows it.

Don't do it all at once. One small action beats a one-hour lecture.

Say it differently

Don't

“How could you believe that? I already told you.”

“No phone for you.”

Try

“Scams are engineered. It isn't about being stupid. Let's look at what's weird together.”

“Hang up. Let's call 165 together and check.”

People who get scammed aren't less smart. In that moment, their emotions were hijacked. What we practise is the pause.

06 / About us

Online safety, made approachable.

SusOrSafe started with a simple frustration: most cyber safety advice is either a scare poster or a compliance slide. We wanted something a family could play together — and something an adult would actually finish.

01

Why we built it

Online risks can be hard to recognize. We built SusorSafe to help beginners practice safer choices through play.

02

What we believe

    Everyone deserves to understand online safety. Learning should be clear, engaging and welcoming, whatever your age or experience.

03

Our goal

Help you recognize online risks, know what to do next, and share what you learn with the people around you.

Online safety starts with one lesson. Learn at your own pace, and share what you learn with family and friends.
